# AKLA due diligence house style

Formatting is the easy half. A volume that looks right but reads wrong still gets
sent back. These conventions are drawn from the firm's circulated volumes and are
what a partner is checking for.

Cleaning up a draft means bringing it to these conventions **without changing what
it says**. Where the draft's substance is wrong or thin, flag it; do not rewrite the
analysis and do not invent findings.

## Voice

Third person throughout: "the Firm", never "we", "I" or "you". The client is "the
Client", the company under review "the Target Company", the deal "the Transaction".

British spelling: authorised, favour, licence (noun), organisation, programme.

Every factual statement cites its source, in the firm's forms:

- "As per section 130(1) of the Companies Act, …"
- "As per Clause 3.1 of the [Instrument], …"
- "The Form A records …" / "Recital C records …"
- "By letter no. [ref] dated [date], …"

Cross-references inside the report: "Please refer to Section 5.4 above.",
"Please refer to Section 4.1 of this Volume I.", "addressed in Volume III (Material
Contracts) of this Report."

## The three building blocks

**Numbered factual paragraphs** state what the law requires and what the documents
record. Neutral, sourced, no argument.

**AKLA Comment** is the Firm's observation: a discrepancy between documents, a gap,
a legal consequence, or a transaction implication. 60 to 140 words. The standing
formulae, used verbatim:

- "The Firm has currently not been provided with [X]. Therefore, the Firm is at
  present unable to comment on [Y]."
- "The Firm notes that [X] … The Firm further notes that [Y]."
- "The Firm is at present unable to reconcile the two."
- "The Firm suggests that this be addressed as a step to completion of the
  Transaction."
- "[X] does not, in itself, evidence [Y]."

**AKLA Action Item** is a single request, always in this shape: "The Target Company
is requested to please provide [documents], together with [related evidence], and to
confirm [fact], for the Firm's review." 30 to 80 words. Requests to the client are
"The Client is requested to please …". Bundle related asks as (a), (b), (c) within
one item. Never phrase a request as a question.

## Numbers, dates, money

| Convention | Example |

## Defined terms

Capitalised, defined once in the Glossary, then used consistently. Do not coin a
second name for something already defined. Glossary entries take one of two forms:

- "Means the finance agreement dated March 31, 2014 entered into by and between the
  Target Company and OPIC, as amended from time to time."
- "Has the meaning ascribed thereto in the Important Note of this Report."

Statutes are defined too: "the Companies Act, 2017 (as amended from time to time)"
becomes "Companies Act". Statutory sections stay lowercase — "section 197" — while
report sections are capitalised — "Section 5.4".

## Analytical habits

These are what make a volume useful, and their absence is the most common substantive
problem in a draft:

- **Reconcile the same fact across documents.** Share capital in the Memorandum
  against the Form A against the audited accounts; dates in a licence against its
  modification. Record every inconsistency as a Comment, however small.
- **Distinguish what a document proves from what it merely recites.**
- **Where a document is missing**, say so, say what the Firm therefore cannot
  confirm, and raise an Action Item. Never leave a gap silent.
- **Identify statutory consequences**, including filing deadlines: "within fifteen
  (15) days", "supported by an affidavit on stamp paper attested by an oath
  commissioner".
- **Flag what falls due at completion** and suggest it be "addressed as a step to
  completion".
- **Never invent facts, dates, figures or clause references.** Where the source is
  illegible, say so: "the date of certification not being legible thereon". A clearly
  printed figure that simply lacks a second source is uncorroborated, not illegible.

## Precision (from the firm's September 14, 2026 drafting review)

Flag these when auditing; fix them only when asked to correct the register.

- **A conclusion no stronger than its evidence.** A board decision evidences the decision,
  not its implementation; a proposal in minutes is not an adopted route; a filing does not
  prove everything it recites.
- **Arithmetic is not legal effect.** Keep a reconciliation, but do not decide which
  conflicting instrument is operative, or that a difference "does not affect" anything,
  because one version adds up.
- **Evidence status is not materiality.** A missing document is a limitation, not by itself a
  breach or a condition precedent.
- **Absent, not found, and not reviewed differ.** A provision not found in an incomplete
  extract is not a provision the instrument lacks, and a document not yet reviewed is not a
  document "not provided".
- **Requests and advice are separate.** An Action Item asks the Target Company for evidence;
  advice to the Client on risk allocation or transaction documents is not an Action Item.
  Each Action Item should be closeable on its own.
- **Annexure A is the reviewed set.** List only documents actually reviewed; a placeholder
  stays a placeholder.

## Scope

Scope is set per engagement, and the report and its Important Note must agree. The firm's
standing Important Note disclaims tax, technical, environmental, insurance and financial
matters, and says labour law is not detailed. Where a volume strays into those, the
convention is to note the point and recommend confirmation by the relevant expert
rather than to opine.
