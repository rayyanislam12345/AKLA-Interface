# Document-type checklists

Read only the section matching the document in front of you.

**This is a reading checklist, not an output template.** It tells you what to
hunt for so nothing material escapes you - including the items that are
findings by their absence. What reaches the memo is the four to six things that
pass the selection test in SKILL.md, each said in a sentence or two. Never
render one of these lists into the document - the memo's short bullets are for
dates and missing items, not for reproducing this checklist.

---

## Contracts and agreements

Read the definitions and schedules before the operative clauses. A defined term
can reverse the plain meaning of the clause that uses it.

- **Parties and capacity** - correct legal names, incorporation details, who
  signs and under what authority. A party named inconsistently across the
  document is a real defect, not a typo.
- **Conditions precedent** - each one, who bears it, whether it is presently
  satisfiable, and what happens on long-stop.
- **Consideration** - amount, currency, timing, escalation, set-off rights,
  withholding and gross-up, late payment interest.
- **Term and renewal** - initial term, auto-renewal, notice to prevent renewal
  (a short window here is a High finding).
- **Termination** - each trigger, each way. Separate termination for cause,
  for convenience, and for insolvency. Note cure periods and their length.
- **Liability** - the cap, what sits outside it, exclusions of consequential
  and indirect loss, and whether the carve-outs swallow the cap.
- **Indemnities** - scope, who gives them, whether capped, conduct-of-claims,
  and whether they survive termination.
- **Warranties and representations** - who gives what, qualified by knowledge
  or materiality, time limits on claims, disclosure against them.
- **Security and guarantees** - what is secured, ranking, enforcement events.
- **Change of control and assignment** - consent rights, deemed-assignment
  language, whether the counterparty can assign freely while the client cannot.
- **Restrictive covenants** - non-compete, non-solicit, exclusivity; duration,
  geography, and whether enforceable in the governing-law jurisdiction.
- **Confidentiality and IP** - ownership of created IP, licences granted, what
  survives.
- **Force majeure** - trigger events, notice, allocation of risk, termination
  on prolonged event.
- **Dispute resolution** - governing law, forum or seat, arbitration rules,
  language, escalation steps, whether interim relief is preserved.
- **Boilerplate that bites** - entire agreement, no-waiver, unilateral
  amendment or variation rights, notice mechanics (address, method, deemed
  receipt), counterparts and electronic execution.
- **Execution** - signature blocks completed, witnessing, stamping and
  registration where the jurisdiction requires it, date of execution vs
  effective date.

**Absences to flag:** no governing law, no cap, no termination right for the
client, no notice provision, referenced schedule not attached, blanks left in
the form ("[●]", "TBD", "____").

## Judgments and case law

- **Court, bench, citation, date, and status** - final or appealable, whether
  any appeal is pending or time to appeal still runs.
- **Parties and posture** - who appealed from what, standard of review.
- **Facts** - only those the court treated as material.
- **Issues framed** - as the court framed them, not as the parties argued them.
- **Holding on each issue**, with the paragraph number.
- **Ratio vs obiter** - mark which is which. Getting this wrong is how bad
  advice gets written.
- **Reasoning** - the chain, and the authorities relied on or distinguished.
- **Orders made** - relief, costs, directions, timelines.
- **Precedential weight** - binding on which courts; any dissent and its
  reasoning; whether it overrules or departs from earlier authority.
- **Application** - how it bears on the matter at hand, stated separately from
  what the judgment says.

## Statutes and regulations

- **Instrument, section numbers, commencement date**, and whether the version
  read is current or amended since.
- **Application** - who and what it binds, territorial scope, thresholds and
  exemptions that decide whether the client is caught at all.
- **Obligations** - each duty, on whom, by when.
- **Prohibitions** and the conduct they capture.
- **Approvals, filings and licences** - authority, form, fee, timeline.
- **Penalties** - fines, imprisonment, civil liability, licence consequences.
- **Enforcement** - the regulator, its powers, appeal route.
- **Transitional and savings provisions** - frequently decisive and frequently
  missed.
- **Cross-references** to other instruments that must be read alongside.

## Due diligence reports

- **Scope and limitations first** - period covered, documents reviewed,
  assumptions, and expressly excluded areas. Everything below is only as good
  as this.
- **Findings by volume/sector**, each graded to the severity rubric.
- **Deal-breakers**, isolated at the top.
- **Conditions to closing** the report implies.
- **Outstanding items** - what was requested and not provided, and the effect
  of the gap. Unanswered requests are findings.
- **Resolved vs open** where the report is a revision of an earlier one.
- **Title, encumbrance and litigation position** in one place - it is what the
  client asks about first.

## Opinions and memoranda

- **Question asked** and the conclusion, in the first two lines.
- **Assumptions and qualifications** the opinion rests on, and which are
  fragile.
- **Reasoning chain** and authorities.
- **Reliance** - who may rely, any cap or disclaimer.
- **Gaps** - questions raised and left unanswered.

---

## Pleadings and filings

- **Cause title** - court, case number, parties, nature of proceeding.
- **Relief sought**, each prayer.
- **Causes of action** and the facts pleaded for each element.
- **Admissions and denials** - a defence's admissions are the most useful lines
  in it.
- **Limitation** - whether the claim appears time-barred on the pleaded facts.
- **Jurisdiction and maintainability** points available.
- **Interim relief** sought or granted, and its conditions.
- **Next date and directions outstanding.**

---

## Notices and correspondence

- **Sender, recipient, date, and delivery method** - and whether it complies
  with the notice clause of the underlying contract. A defective notice is
  often the whole answer.
- **What it asserts** and what it demands.
- **The clock it starts** - cure period, deadline to respond, and the computed
  date.
- **Consequences stated** if the deadline passes.
- **Reservation of rights**, or its absence.
- **What a response must cover** to protect the client's position.
